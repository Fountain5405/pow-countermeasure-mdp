## 3. Methods

- **PTO** (paper 4.3; `blockchain_mdps/base/solver/pto_solver.py`): multiply each transition's probability by (1-1/H)^D(s,a,s'), cut all mass into the final state (`:39-40` dense, `:50-55` sparse), solve the resulting SSP with `mdptoolbox.PolicyIteration` (`:24`, discount=1, matrix evaluation), return policy and `V[initial]/H` (`:30`). Defaults H=1e5, epsilon=1e-5, max_iter=1e5 (`:15-19`). Policy quality is then measured by `calc_policy_revenue` (steady state of the original chain, `blockchain_mdp.py:90-100`; sparse = ARPACK `eigs(P.T, k=1, which='LR')`, `sparse_blockchain_mdp.py:25`).
- **OSM** (`osm_solver.py`): Sapirshtein et al. bisection on rho in [0,1] to `epsilon/8` (`:27`), each step a regenerative MDP with reward (1-rho)R - rho(D-R), transitions into the initial state and the final state zeroed (`:63-65`), decision `V[init] > 0` (`:43`); returns rho, not an evaluated revenue. Illegal actions keep their penalty in the expected reward here (`:60` uses unmodified P), so OSM handles them correctly; PTO does not.
- **ContinuousOSM** (`continuous_osm_solver.py`): OSM warm-started with the previous policy (`policy0`). **SMDP** (`smdp_solver.py`): Dinkelbach-style iteration, reward R - rev*D, rev = evaluated policy revenue each round, stop when |drev| <= epsilon (`:138`). Neither is described in the paper; `main_mdp_comparison.py` compares PTO/OSM/COSM/SMDP at alpha=.35, gamma=0, H=1e5, eps=1e-5, max_fork 5..100 (output files not in repo).
- **PI-OSM** (the paper's OSM with policy iteration, MATLAB, from Ren Zhang's code) is not in this repo.
- **RL** (`reinforcement_learning/`, WeRLman-era): `MDPBlockchainSimulator` samples the same `get_state_transitions` and terminates via the final state; agents are LDDQN (lookahead difference DQN, depth d, value discount (1-1/H)^D per transition, reward/H, `lddqn_agent.py:61-75`), LSMDQN (`lookahead_sm_dqn/`, reward - base_value*D), DQN, and MCTSTrainer (PUCT, difference DQN, `mcts_difference_dqn/`). Common: base value approximation (BVA), 256x256 MLP, Huber loss, multi-process synchronized training, TensorBoard. Typical args (`main_rl.py`): H=1e4, depth 5-10, lr 2e-4, nn_factor 1e-3, 5 train + 2 eval agents; MCTS with 25 simulations, 5001 epochs. Policies are re-evaluated exactly (`log_solution_info`, `main_rl.py:36-42`) and compared to `PTOSolver(epsilon=1e-7)` exact revenue (`solve_mdp_exactly`, `:28-33`). RL gives lower bounds on the optimum only. The simulator marks an action illegal only when its penalty is exactly `error_penalty` (`mdp_blockchain_simulator.py:157`), so the `error_penalty/2` and `/4` cases count as legal.
- **Stated guarantees** (paper): Theorem 1, |REV_ARR^pi - REV_PT^pi(H)| = O(1/H) for any policy, assuming bounded |R| <= Rmax, 0 <= D <= Dmax, average D > epsilon_D, one positive recurrent state. The appendix constant is 3 Dmax^2 eps_D^-2 Rmax/(H-1) (A.3 end); Lemma 4 (A.1): |E[sum D until termination] - H| <= Dmax+1. Corollary 2: max-policy convergence as H -> inf. The paper says the error is "not known in advance" (6.3.1 note). Empirical: H=1e6 "negligible"; stopping thresholds 1e-5 (Bitcoin), 1e-7 (Ethereum H=1e5), 1e-8 (Ethereum H=1e6).
- Complexity: PTO solves ~10 linear systems vs ~140 for PI-OSM (Fig 3b); up to 190k states (max_fork ~250); wall time ~250 s PTO(Python) vs ~360 s PI-OSM vs ~740 s OSM (MATLAB, Fig 3a).

## 5. Limitations and discrepancies bearing on our accuracy findings

1. **Final-state zeroing**: `pto_solver.py:40` / `:55` set P(.,final)=0 after scaling. mdptoolbox builds the per-action expected reward from the *given* P (`mdp.py:316-326`, `P.multiply(R).sum(1)`), so the `error_penalty` reward on illegal transitions is multiplied by 0 and illegal actions become "reward 0, terminate". With nonnegative rewards this is harmless for revenue; for rates below ~1e-6 it breaks (our reviews). The 2020 code did the same (`ae79fe0:base_blockchain_mdp.py get_pt_mdp`). OSM/SMDP/COSM do not have this flaw (penalty enters `r_mat` before zeroing).
2. **Epsilon is not a policy-optimality tolerance**: mdptoolbox-roi `PolicyIteration.run` breaks when `getSpan(V - Vprev) < epsilon` (`mdp.py:877`), an absolute test on V, where V ~ H*g. Epsilon 1e-5 is a 1e-10 relative test at g=0.3, H=1e5 but 1 % at g=1e-8. "Added support for epsilon in policy iteration" is commit 0c9fef5/a8a49d1, 2020-07-10, the day v1 appeared.
3. **Linear solver changed in 2025**: until 2025-05-28 the sparse evaluation was exact `spsolve` (umfpack since 2020-07-10). Since 2025-05-29 (commits 5a43fb9 GMRES, d4e9816 QMR, fc036ef BiCG, acfaa41 fallback chain) it uses Krylov with `atol=1e-3*epsilon`, `rtol=1e-7`, `x0=self.V` (`mdp.py:832-840`). Any numbers made with 4.2.0 differ in kind from the paper's. Our venv runs 4.2.0 if installed from PyPI today; version pinning is needed. MAD-DAG says it also moved to BiCG (2025).
4. **Horizon bias is in V/H, not in the evaluated policy**: Lemma 4 gives E[sum D] = H +- (Dmax+1), so V[init]/H is off by O(Dmax/H) relative, matching the ~10/H low we saw. The paper's tables and Figs 1-2 report ARR-evaluated policies, so they do not carry it; `solve_mdp_exactly` also evaluates. The Theorem 1 constant (3 Dmax^2 eps_D^-2 Rmax/(H-1)) is vacuous at Dmax = 40-200, H = 1e4-1e5; the paper only argues empirically.
5. **Assumption 1 is violated by the code**: Rmax is finite only if error-penalty rewards are excluded; the proof assumes they never occur. Penalties are therefore outside the theorem even before zeroing.
6. **Truncation**: Fig 2 shows the revenue near alpha=.45 needs max_fork >= ~50, and the paper argues "exponentially declining" tail so 100 suffices. That is a revenue argument. For rare-event objectives (reorgs of depth >= 11 at alpha 0.25: rates ~1e-8) the truncation loss is not negligible relative to the rate (roughly (alpha/(1-alpha))^k per fork length).
7. `calc_policy_revenue` assumes a unichain policy (ARPACK 'LR' eigenvector, `null_space` in the dense case) and zeroes `final->final`; a policy with several recurrent classes or an illegal action reached gives an eigenvector that is not the stationary law. No check in the code.
8. Stale scripts: `main.py` imports a deleted module (`blockchain_mdps.bitcoin_mdp`); `main_mdp_threshold.py` `model_creator` uses undefined `max_fork`, `gamma` and an irrelevant `acceptable_path_param` sweep (NameError); `main_mdp_comparison.py` uses `DataFrame.append` (removed in pandas 2). Its threshold test is `rev > honest + margin` with margin = max(1e-3, eps) (same class of tolerance issue as ZP's +1e-4 margin). No tests in the repo.
9. OSM comparison bug noted by the authors: hard-coded iteration cap in the original OSM code made published OSM values low at alpha >= .425, gamma 0, mf 95 (Table 1). Any SSZ-style table from that code at high alpha is a lower bound with error up to ~1e-4.
10. Repo/paper differences: Table 1's PTO column uses H=1e6; `main_mdp.py` uses H=1e4, eps 1e-5, max_fork 200 on the bare fee model (PT value and ARR value both printed, they differ).

## 6. Coverage in pcmdp

Vendored (`pcmdp/vendor/VENDOR.md`, matches 89c4086 apart from imports and networkx names, verified by diff): all of `base/` (spaces, `BlockchainMDP`/`SparseBlockchainMDP`, PTO/OSM/COSM/SMDP solvers, `StateTransitions`) and `BitcoinModel`. Used: `BitcoinModel` as `NakamotoModel` base, `SparseBlockchainMDP` matrices (`howard.py:_tables`), PTOSolver only as a cross-check in tests (`tests/test_countermeasures.py:26`, `test_howard.py:18`, `test_sop_walk.py:23`) and `solve.py:106-117` (pto/osm choice). Replaced by us: PTOSolver by exact Howard iteration (`howard.py`); `calc_policy_revenue` by the stationary solve in `reorgs.reorg_profile`.

Dropped on purpose: Ethereum, Simple, all fee models, the whole RL package, the OSM-comparison scripts.

Missing, relative to the gist's goals:
- **Reorg-depth output** and **disruption objective**: pto has neither; pcmdp built them (`reorgs.py`). Nothing to import.
- **Uncle/orphan-reward machinery** (Ethereum model: au/hu/r bookkeeping, difficulty counting uncles, negative rewards): the only ready-made template for countermeasures that pay or count orphans (reward splitting, subchains, fruitchains already ported from ZP/Aumayr). Port only if a Monero-relevant uncle-style proposal enters scope.
- **Variable block rewards (fees, whales, MEV)**: `BitcoinSimplifiedFeeModel` / `BitcoinBareFeeModel`; needed only if the revenue objective must include transaction fees (WeRLman, MAD-DAG claim that heavy tails move thresholds a lot).
- **RL/MCTS for state explosion** (exact SoP game, 1.2 M-state tight model at 14 GB, Ethereum-scale spaces): the repo's LDDQN/MCTS + exact re-evaluation is the only reference. It yields lower bounds only; pair with our over-paying upper bound. Needs torch, 5+ processes, long runs: lane box only, and optional.
- **Final-state semantics**: our models follow pto's `error_penalty -> final` convention (PORTING.md); Howard drops illegal actions, PTO/Table comparisons must not use PTOSolver for tiny rates.
- Solver options not mirrored: OSM-style `rho` bisection exists only through the vendored OSMSolver, whose `V[init] > 0` test is also absolute-tolerance mdptoolbox policy iteration.

## 7. What should change in how we run or compare

1. Compare to published numbers with the ARR-evaluated optimum (our Howard `g`), never `V/H`. Table 1 (alpha .35-.475, gamma 0, mf 95) is a ready oracle for `NakamotoModel`; expect agreement to <= 1e-5 (PTO column), and expect our values to be ABOVE the OSM column by up to 1.2e-4 at alpha >= .425. `tests/test_countermeasures.py:66` checks only alpha .35. Ethereum Tables 2-3 have no pcmdp counterpart.
2. Pin and record `pymdptoolbox-roi` version in every run provenance; treat <= 4.1.x (exact spsolve) and >= 4.2.0 (Krylov, atol 1e-3*eps) as different solvers. Keep PTOSolver only in tests with a floor: do not assert below g ~ 1e-6.
3. When citing external PTO-based thresholds (MAD-DAG, WeRLman), note H=1e5-1e6, eps 1e-5 to 1e-8, and that thresholds come from `rev > honest + margin`; at the threshold the excess is ~1e-5, the same size as the solver tolerance (ZP's issue repeats).
4. Disruption/rare-event runs: report the cap used, and check cap sensitivity at the target rate, not at revenue (limitation 6). Add a unichain/stationarity check before reading `reorg_profile`.
5. Tag illegal-action handling in each ported model with a test that the `error_penalty` transition is never in the optimal policy (cheap guard against limitation 1).
6. Optional: vendor `EthereumModel` and the simplified fee model only if the gist's countermeasure list needs orphan rewards or variable rewards; skip RL unless an exact solve is infeasible.
